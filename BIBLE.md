# AFTERFLOW — Project Bible v1.2 (binding, set by Tanvir 22 Oct 2026, updated 23 Oct 2026)
Supersedes PRD.md wherever they conflict. Changes need Tanvir's approval.

## 0. Positioning
Afterflow keeps the door open. You two walk through it.
In Tanvir's words: Afterflow is "a connector, not the connection itself; the connecting is done between the individuals." It gets two people who met to the app they already talk on, and then gets out of the way.
Feature test: does it get two people talking somewhere else, faster? If not, it doesn't ship.
Out: a built-in chat or messaging, feeds, and browsable profiles.

## 1. Vision
Help people who meet in real life keep casually interacting afterwards, until strangers become familiar, and familiar can become friendship.
Promise: Keep the people you meet in your life.
Starts with hackathons and similar events. Friendship is the primary outcome. Networking, collaboration, jobs and co-founding are possible secondary outcomes, never requirements.

## 2. Problem
Conversation is natural during an event because of the shared environment; afterwards it disappears. Exchanging contacts doesn't create a relationship. Reaching out to someone you barely know feels awkward and gets harder over time. The problem is not forgetting names; it is losing natural opportunities to interact repeatedly.

## 3. Insight
People don't become friends because an algorithm says they're compatible. They become friends through repeated time, conversation and shared experience.
Progression (not guaranteed): Met -> Familiar -> Comfortable -> Friends.

## 4. Audience
Initial: hackathon, builder meetup and tech event attendees. Future: university societies, conferences, coworking/founder networks, online communities that meet IRL, people new to a city. Start with one audience.

## 5. Principles (every feature must respect)
1. Friendship before networking.
2. Low effort.
3. Repeated interaction, not storing contacts.
4. Natural, not forced. No manufactured intimacy, no guilt for not responding.
5. Mutual participation. No unsolicited access.
6. Fit into existing behaviour/platforms.
7. No obligation to achieve anything.
8. Quality over quantity.

## 6. Core experience
1 Meet. 2 Stay connected (quick, mutual, no lengthy profiles/onboarding). 3 Make future interaction easy (casual conversation, shared updates, lightweight prompts, community activity, invitations; mechanism is an OPEN decision). 4 Become familiar. 5 Let the relationship develop; Afterflow does not prescribe the outcome.

## 7. Defining question (every feature)
Does this make it easier for two people who have met to interact again and gradually become more familiar with each other?
If a feature only collects data, boosts engagement metrics, or creates professional matches without improving that, it is not a priority.

## 8. Initial scope
Essential:
- Simple way to establish a connection after meeting.
- Lightweight way to find and revisit connections.
- Mechanism encouraging natural, low-pressure repeat interaction.
- Clear route to continue on an existing platform.
- Simple way to leave, mute or stop a connection without social pressure.
Not required: WhatsApp/Discord replacement; general social network; public follower counts/rankings; AI compatibility scoring; professional networking marketplace; complex profiles, feeds or messaging infra; guaranteed outcomes.

## 9. Integrations
Complement, don't replace. WhatsApp: 1:1 continuation via contact sharing/hand-offs; never assume reading chats. Discord: opt-in bots/channels/activities respecting permissions and consent. Luma: entry point / event context, not the core. Don't build all at once; validate capability, access and demand first.

## 10. Success
North star: meaningful continued interactions between people who met through an event/community.
Supporting: % connections with a second interaction; % still interacting after 1 week / 1 month; mutually acknowledged repeat interactions; voluntary continued use after event; self-reported familiarity.
Do NOT optimise for sign-ups, contacts collected, messages generated or time in app.

## 11. Risks -> responses
Dead social network -> specific community + clear reason to return. Forced interaction -> voluntary, easy-to-ignore prompts. "People have WhatsApp" -> solve continuity between meeting and messaging, don't rebuild messaging. Both must join -> minimise onboarding, test one-sided utility first. Activity != friendship -> measure continued, mutually welcome interaction, not volume.

## 12. Open decisions (resolve by exploration, not assumption)
1 Smallest interaction that makes two people likelier to talk again? 2 Small event groups, individual connections, or both? 3 Main mechanism: shared activity, light prompts, recurring encounters, other? 4 Day-one-after-event experience? 5 Best first platform for distribution/interaction? 6 Value if only one person uses it? 7 Evidence of growing familiarity vs just messages?

## 13. Standard
Success: someone returns to ordinary life after an event and finds it easier to keep talking to the people they met. No demands to become professional contacts, join noisy communities, maintain profiles, or join artificial engagement loops. The product exists to preserve the possibility of friendship after the circumstances that created it have disappeared.
